Some thoughts on data availability, inspired by [blog](https://interlatent.com/blog/interlatent-modern-ai-robotics-first-principles)

I am going into the field of neural engineering, and I want to work on software. We desperately want some major progress in a brain foundation model. To achieve this, one of the approaches is to blend together different data modalities (EEG, MEG, fMRI, etc). Each modality has some tradeoff and to see the clearest picture, we should develop a model with the capacity to stitch together all these modalities. 

Crucially, data is the issue. Unlike language, where data is structured and abundant (via the internet), neural recordings are different in length, drift across sessions and people, and are generally much more scarce. These are independent problems. 

However, neural recordings also isn't the only field that suffers from this problem. The path to a robot mind also suffers similarly. There has been 2 paths:
1. Human data collection via teleoperation
	1. hard to scale, since each hour of data collected takes 1 hour of human time. This type of data however, is the highest quality
2. simulation
	1. simulating across distributions of physical parameters such that the real world, with deployment error, fits within the trained distribution. Examples such as NVidia Issac sim or omniverse. 

What can we learn via the data collection process? Both of these are approaches we can take for neural recordings. Though human data collection is painfully hard to get and difficult to scale, it is nonetheless a starting point. What I'm more interested in, is simulation. 

The physical world, given human's ability to interpret it, is relatively easy to verify. The brain, not so much. How can we develop better simulations? 

Basically, I think we can learn a lot about data collection and simulation from the robotics world.
