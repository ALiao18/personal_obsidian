# Brains are not Computers
Luis H. Favela
Department of History and Philosophy of science and medicine, Indiana University Bloomington

_Motivation:_
we implicitly assume brain is a computer. Psychology, neuroscience, etc also takes it as a truism. Also AI (Marvin Minsky). 

_Important to distinguish computers_
1. computer_person: a human being who does calculations
Mereological fallacy: computation only happens with brains -> actually, its the whole system (person) doing the work
2. computer_turing: classical turing machine
Not a turing machine because 1. generality: we seem to entertain infinite object matters and combinations of sentences. A turing machine requires a 1-1 map of mind state and machine state. It is unlikely our brain is that capable of storing. 
3. computer_network: connectionism -> large scale of simple perceptrons 
we don't really even understand biological neurons yet. We can't assume the metaphor. Also, ANNs don't have the same operating principles human brains do. 
4. computer_metaphor: the brain is metaphorically like a computer
People mistake analogy for metaphor. 

_If not a computer, what is it?_
Eukaryotic organisms have no brains, but do computation (e.g they can solve mazes). Insects have very simple brains, but do quite complicated complication (e.g navigation, waggle dance, etc). 
Planaria has concentrated brain in the head, but if you give it a task, cut it up (below the brain), the bottom half will also grow a brain out, and it will still remember that task. 
Mammals like Mike the headless chicken: head chopped off, but it lived for another 18 months. The farmer likely cut right at the brain stem. What is the role of the brain in that case?

What are viable alternatives?
1. complex dynamical system
2. coordinate system
3. control system

Intelligence as a coordinate function. An "intelligent" event is a coordination in latent space that chains together functions.
